# Go-to-Market Strategy

A go-to-market (GTM) strategy is the plan for launching a product into a market and acquiring customers. Marketers write this before a product launch, market expansion, or major repositioning. It brings together market definition, customer targeting, channel strategy, pricing, and launch execution into a single plan. It answers: "How do we get this product into the hands of the right customers, at the right price, through the right channels?"

## Before you start

- **Product context.** This framework needs to know the product: name, what it does, who it serves. Take it from the request, the project's CLAUDE.md or README, or earlier conversation. If it is missing and matters for the output, ask one short question instead of inventing facts.
- **Language.** The framework is written in English; write the deliverable in the language of the request (Russian request → Russian output), and adapt examples, platforms and conventions to that market.
- **No invented numbers.** Placeholders in `[brackets]` are to be filled with real data from the user. Where data is missing, leave a clearly marked placeholder rather than fabricating metrics, quotes or customer names.

## How to Write Each Section

**Executive Summary**
Write this last, even though it appears first. Summarize the launch in 3-5 sentences: what you're launching, who it's for, why the timing is right, and the core bet you're making. An executive should be able to read this section alone and understand the strategy.

Good: "We're launching Meridian Analytics, a marketing attribution platform for B2B SaaS companies ($10M-$100M ARR) that can't connect marketing spend to pipeline. The market is $2.3B and growing at 18% as privacy changes break legacy attribution. Our bet: mid-market SaaS companies will pay $49-199/mo for attribution that's 80% as powerful as Bizible at 20% of the cost, if we can deliver the first report within 14 days."

Bad: "We're launching a great new product that will revolutionize marketing analytics." (No specifics, no market context, no strategy.)

**Market Definition**
TAM/SAM/SOM gives leadership a clear picture of the opportunity. TAM is everyone who could buy. SAM is everyone you can reach with current capabilities. SOM is what you can realistically capture in year one. Be honest about SOM — a $10B TAM means nothing if your SOM is $500K.

**Ideal Customer Profile**
The ICP is the single most important section of the GTM. If you get this wrong, everything downstream (channels, messaging, pricing, sales enablement) misses. Be specific: company profile, decision maker, pain point, current solution, buying trigger, budget, and sales cycle. Equally important: define who is NOT a fit. The disqualified profiles section saves sales time and marketing budget.

**Pricing Strategy**
Price communicates value. Document your tiers, what's included, and who each tier serves. Include the rationale: are you pricing to undercut a competitor? Pricing to value? Pricing to maximize adoption? Be explicit. Include launch promotions and annual discounting strategy.

**Channel Strategy**
Identify 3-5 acquisition channels, map each to a funnel stage, and prioritize ruthlessly. Name 1-2 channels to "bet big on" — these get the most budget and attention. Name 1-2 to "test" — smaller experiments. Name channels to avoid and why. Spreading budget evenly across 8 channels produces mediocre results everywhere.

**Launch Plan**
Break the launch into three phases: pre-launch (building assets, teasing, warming audiences), launch (day-of activities across all channels), and post-launch (optimization, follow-up, scaling what works). Every activity needs an owner and a due date.

**Sales Enablement**
List every asset the sales team needs, with owners and due dates. Include objection handling — the top 3-5 objections with specific, proof-backed responses. Sales teams that can't handle objections lose deals that marketing worked hard to create.

## Conventions

- Length: 5-10 pages
- Tone: Strategic and execution-focused. This is both a strategy document and an operational plan
- One GTM per product launch or major market expansion — don't combine multiple launches in one document
- TAM/SAM/SOM must include sources — unsourced market sizing is fiction
- The ICP must be specific enough for a sales rep to qualify a lead in 5 minutes
- Include disqualified profiles — they prevent wasted sales time and marketing spend
- Channel strategy must include prioritization — betting equally on everything is betting on nothing
- Launch plan activities need owners and dates — plans without accountability are wishlists
- Sales enablement assets should be listed with due dates and tracked against the launch timeline
- Include a risk section — every launch has failure modes. Naming them enables faster response
- Update this document weekly during pre-launch and launch phases
- Review success metrics at 30, 60, and 90 days post-launch — adjust the GTM based on what you learn

## Anti-Patterns

- Do NOT launch without defining the ICP — "everyone who does marketing" is not an ICP
- Do NOT skip the disqualified profiles section — without it, sales will chase bad-fit leads and waste cycles
- Do NOT spread budget equally across all channels — focus beats diversification in early-stage launches
- Do NOT set revenue targets without backing into them — "$1M ARR in year 1" needs a bottoms-up model showing how many leads, at what conversion rate, at what price point
- Do NOT write a GTM without a launch date — open-ended strategies never launch
- Do NOT confuse a GTM with a business plan — the GTM focuses on the first 90-180 days of taking a product to market, not on 5-year financial projections
- Do NOT ignore the status quo as a competitor — for most B2B products, "do nothing" is the biggest competitor
- Do NOT plan sales enablement after launch — sales teams need battle cards, one-pagers, and objection handling before the first lead comes in
- Do NOT set metrics you can't actually measure — if you don't have attribution tracking set up, "content-sourced pipeline" is not a measurable metric on day one
- Do NOT launch on more than 2 channels simultaneously if your team is under 5 people — you'll execute poorly on all of them
- Do NOT price without competitive context — your price only makes sense relative to what the buyer compares you to
- Do NOT write a GTM and skip the post-launch phase — the plan for what happens after launch day determines whether you scale or stall
- Do NOT treat partnerships as "nice to have" — for many B2B products, integration and channel partnerships are the primary acquisition engine

## Examples

```markdown
# Go-to-Market Strategy: StackPlan — Project Estimation for Engineering Teams

**Author:** Derek Huang
**Last Updated:** 2026-03-20
**Launch Date:** 2026-05-12
**Status:** Pre-Launch

---

## 1. Executive Summary

StackPlan is a project estimation tool that helps engineering managers produce accurate sprint estimates by analyzing historical velocity data, task complexity patterns, and team capacity. We're targeting engineering managers at B2B SaaS companies (50-300 employees) who consistently miss sprint deadlines because estimation is done by gut feel. The estimation software market is $890M and growing at 16% CAGR, driven by the shift to data-driven engineering practices. Our bet: engineering managers will pay $15/seat/month for a tool that integrates with Jira and Linear and reduces estimation error from 40% (industry average) to under 15%, because missed estimates cost them credibility with product and leadership.

## 2. Market Definition

### Market Size
- **TAM:** $890M — global project estimation and planning software
- **SAM:** $210M — B2B SaaS engineering teams in the US and Europe using Jira or Linear
- **SOM:** $2.1M — 1,000 teams at $15/seat/month with an average of 12 seats, captured in year 1

### Market Trends
- Engineering teams are shifting from intuition-based to data-driven estimation, driven by the broader DevOps metrics movement (DORA, SPACE frameworks)
- Jira and Linear are becoming the default project management layer, creating a standardized data source for estimation intelligence
- "Developer productivity" is a C-suite topic for the first time — McKinsey's 2025 developer productivity report accelerated executive interest

### Timing Rationale
Engineering managers are under unprecedented pressure to demonstrate predictability. The shift to data-driven engineering practices has created demand for estimation tools, but the market is underserved — most teams use spreadsheets or Planning Poker. Jira's API improvements in 2025 made real-time velocity analysis feasible for the first time at scale.

## 3. Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

### Primary ICP
- **Company Profile:** B2B SaaS, 50-300 employees, Series A-C, $5M-$50M ARR
- **Decision Maker:** Engineering Manager or VP of Engineering
- **Pain Point:** Sprints consistently miss their commitments by 30-50%. Product loses trust in engineering estimates. The eng manager spends 4+ hours per sprint planning using gut feel
- **Current Solution:** Planning Poker + spreadsheets + Jira velocity reports (which they don't trust because the data is messy)
- **Buying Trigger:** A missed deadline on a high-stakes project — a launch delay, a partner commitment broken, or a board-level question about engineering predictability
- **Budget:** $500-$3,000/month for productivity tools (this competes with Jira add-ons, not major platform spend)
- **Sales Cycle:** 14-30 days for teams under 50 engineers. PLG motion — self-serve signup, 14-day trial, convert to paid

### Secondary ICP
- **Company Profile:** Mid-market SaaS, 300-1,000 employees, Series C-E
- **Decision Maker:** Director of Engineering or VP of Engineering
- **Pain Point:** Multiple teams with inconsistent estimation practices. Cannot forecast delivery timelines for quarterly planning
- **Current Solution:** Custom internal dashboards built by eng ops + Jira plugins
- **Buying Trigger:** Quarterly planning fails because delivery forecasts were wrong — executive visibility into engineering predictability becomes a priority
- **Budget:** $3,000-$10,000/month
- **Sales Cycle:** 45-90 days, requires a champion (eng manager) plus an approver (VP Eng)

### Disqualified Profiles
- Agencies and consultancies — their project structures are per-client, not per-sprint. Our data model doesn't fit
- Companies not using Jira or Linear — we require one of these for data integration. No manual data entry
- Teams under 5 engineers — not enough velocity data for meaningful estimation improvement

## 4. Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Positioning | Pricing | Our Advantage |
|-----------|-------------|---------|--------------|
| Jellyfish | "Engineering management platform" | $25/seat/mo (enterprise sales) | We're focused solely on estimation, not full EM platform — faster to deploy, cheaper, deeper on estimation accuracy |
| LinearB | "Dev pipeline delivery intelligence" | $20/seat/mo | LinearB focuses on cycle time and workflow metrics. We focus on forward-looking estimation accuracy — different value prop |
| Jira native velocity reports | Built-in estimation tools | $0 (included with Jira) | Jira velocity reports are backward-looking averages. We use ML to account for task complexity, team composition, and historical patterns |
| Planning Poker + spreadsheets | Manual estimation in sprint planning | $0 + 4 hrs/sprint | We reduce estimation time from 4 hours to 30 minutes with data-backed suggestions, and improve accuracy from ~60% to 85%+ |

## 5. Positioning & Messaging

**Positioning Statement:**
For engineering managers at growing SaaS companies who miss sprint commitments because estimation is based on gut feel, StackPlan is the estimation intelligence tool that predicts sprint outcomes with 85%+ accuracy by analyzing your actual Jira or Linear data. Unlike Jellyfish or manual Planning Poker, we focus entirely on making estimates accurate — no dashboards, no reports, just estimates you can trust, live in 15 minutes.

**Key Messages:**
1. "Stop guessing. Start predicting." — StackPlan analyzes your team's actual velocity data to predict sprint outcomes with 85%+ accuracy
2. Connects to Jira or Linear in 15 minutes. Your first sprint prediction is ready before your next planning meeting
3. Used by 200+ engineering teams. Average estimation accuracy improvement: from 58% to 87% within 3 sprints

**Tagline:** Estimation intelligence for engineering teams.

## 6. Pricing Strategy

| Tier | Price | What's Included | Target Customer |
|------|-------|----------------|----------------|
| Starter | Free forever | 1 team (up to 10 members), basic estimation, Jira or Linear integration | Small teams validating the tool. PLG entry point |
| Team | $15/seat/month | Unlimited teams, advanced estimation models, historical analysis, Slack integration | Primary ICP: 10-50 engineer teams |
| Enterprise | Custom ($12/seat at volume) | SSO, advanced security, dedicated CSM, API access, custom models | Secondary ICP: 100+ engineer organizations |

**Pricing Rationale:**
$15/seat/month positions us below Jellyfish ($25) and LinearB ($20) while being premium enough to signal quality. The free tier captures PLG adoption and creates a pipeline of teams that upgrade as they grow. At an average of 12 seats, the Team tier generates $180/month per customer — within the budget range for engineering tool discretionary spend (no VP approval needed under $200/mo at most SaaS companies).

**Discounts & Promotions:**
- Launch: 25% off annual plan for first 100 customers (locked for year 1)
- Annual vs. monthly: 20% discount for annual commitment ($12/seat/mo vs. $15)
- No partner or referral program at launch — revisit at 500 customers
```

## Customization

Replace all `[bracketed]` sections with your product specifics. Adjust the number of channels, launch phases, and partnership targets based on team size and budget. A 2-person team should focus on 2-3 channels and a simpler launch plan. The ICP section is the most important — spend the most time here. If your product is PLG, the sales enablement section can be lighter. If your product is sales-led, expand it with discovery call scripts and demo flows. Update this document weekly during pre-launch and launch, then monthly during post-launch.
