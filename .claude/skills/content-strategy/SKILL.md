# Content Strategy

A content strategy defines what you publish, where you publish it, and why. Marketers write this to align content efforts with business goals, ensure consistency across channels, and create a system that compounds over time instead of one-off posts that disappear. It answers: "What content do we create, for whom, on which channels, and how do we know it's working?"

## Before you start

- **Product context.** This framework needs to know the product: name, what it does, who it serves. Take it from the request, the project's CLAUDE.md or README, or earlier conversation. If it is missing and matters for the output, ask one short question instead of inventing facts.
- **Language.** The framework is written in English; write the deliverable in the language of the request (Russian request → Russian output), and adapt examples, platforms and conventions to that market.
- **No invented numbers.** Placeholders in `[brackets]` are to be filled with real data from the user. Where data is missing, leave a clearly marked placeholder rather than fabricating metrics, quotes or customer names.

## How to Write Each Section

**Content Mission Statement**
This is your editorial north star. It should name your audience, the value you provide, and the outcome for your business. Keep it to 2-3 sentences. Test it: if a team member reads it, can they decide whether a content idea fits the strategy?

Good: "We create practical guides and frameworks that help B2B marketers at growing SaaS companies drive pipeline through organic content. Our content builds trust that converts readers into product users."

Bad: "We create great content that drives engagement and thought leadership." (Vague. Everyone says this.)

**Business Goals**
Map each business goal to content's specific role. Content doesn't exist in a vacuum — it serves demand gen, retention, brand awareness, or product adoption. Be explicit about which. If you can't tie a content effort to a business goal, question whether you should be creating it.

**Audience Segments**
Three segments is the sweet spot. More than four means you're spreading too thin. Each segment needs distinct pain points and content preferences — if two segments consume the same content the same way, merge them.

Good: "Mid-market SaaS marketers (50-200 employees) who run a content program with 1-3 people. Their biggest pain: creating enough quality content with limited resources. They prefer tactical, template-driven content they can implement immediately. Active on LinkedIn and Twitter, subscribe to 2-3 industry newsletters."

Bad: "Marketers who want to learn about marketing." (Not actionable.)

**Content Pillars**
Pillars are the 3-5 thematic categories all your content falls into. Every piece you publish should fit under one pillar. Allocate a percentage of total output to each pillar based on audience demand and business priority. Review and adjust these percentages quarterly.

**Channel Mix**
For each channel, define its role in the funnel (awareness, engagement, conversion), the content types that work there, how often you post, and who owns it. A channel without an owner is a channel that dies.

**Measurement Plan**
Separate leading indicators (you can influence weekly) from lagging indicators (show results monthly). Leading: pageviews, social impressions, email open rates. Lagging: MQLs from content, pipeline influence, organic traffic growth. Track both — leading indicators tell you if you're on pace; lagging indicators tell you if it's working.

**Editorial Guidelines**
Set clear quality and SEO standards so every contributor produces consistent work. Include approval workflows — who reviews, who approves, what the turnaround time is.

**Content Repurposing Framework**
Every high-effort piece (blog post, webinar, report) should generate 3-5 derivative pieces for other channels. Plan repurposing at creation time, not after. This is how you scale output without scaling headcount.

## Conventions

- Length: 4-8 pages
- Review and update quarterly — a stale content strategy actively misleads the team
- Tone: Strategic but actionable. Every section should lead to a decision or action
- Include specific numbers for cadence and targets — "regularly" and "often" are not cadences
- Tie every content pillar to a business goal and an audience segment — orphaned pillars waste resources
- Define one primary metric per channel — tracking everything means prioritizing nothing
- Name owners for every channel and approval step — unowned work doesn't happen
- Include a repurposing plan — one-and-done content is the most expensive content you can create
- Use tables for structured data (goals, channels, cadence) and prose for strategy and nuance
- Link to supporting docs (brand voice guide, SEO playbook, buyer personas) instead of duplicating them
- Version the document with a "Last Updated" date so the team knows they're reading current strategy

## Anti-Patterns

- Do NOT write a content strategy without defined audience segments — you'll produce generic content that resonates with nobody
- Do NOT list channels without specifying each one's role — posting everywhere without purpose wastes effort
- Do NOT skip the measurement plan — unmeasured content programs lose budget in the next planning cycle
- Do NOT create more than 5 content pillars — if everything is a pillar, nothing is a priority
- Do NOT set cadence you can't sustain — promising 5 blog posts per week and delivering 2 erodes team trust and content quality
- Do NOT treat all channels equally — 80% of your results will come from 1-2 channels. Identify them and go deep
- Do NOT confuse content types with content pillars — "blog post" is a format, not a pillar. "Product-led growth tactics" is a pillar
- Do NOT write a strategy document and never revisit it — quarterly reviews are the minimum
- Do NOT ignore the content repurposing framework — a 2,000-word blog post that only lives on the blog is 70% wasted effort
- Do NOT plan content in a vacuum without input from sales, product, and customer success — they know what questions prospects and customers actually ask
- Do NOT optimize for volume over quality — 2 excellent posts per week outperform 10 mediocre ones
- Do NOT set measurement targets without baselines — "increase traffic by 30%" means nothing if you don't know where you started
- Do NOT forget to include SEO standards — organic search is the compounding engine of content marketing

## Examples

```markdown
# Content Strategy: Beamline Analytics

**Author:** Priya Nair
**Last Updated:** 2026-03-15
**Review Cadence:** Quarterly (next review: June 2026)

---

## 1. Content Mission Statement

Beamline Analytics creates practical, data-backed content that helps B2B SaaS marketing teams measure what matters, eliminate vanity metrics, and prove content ROI to their leadership. Our content builds trust that converts readers into free trial users and positions us as the authority on marketing analytics.

## 2. Business Goals

| Goal | Content's Role | Metric | Target | Timeframe |
|------|---------------|--------|--------|-----------|
| Generate qualified pipeline | Drive organic MQLs through search-optimized content | Content-sourced MQLs | 150/month | Q3 2026 |
| Reduce CAC | Shift acquisition mix toward organic from paid | Organic as % of total signups | 35% (from 18%) | Q4 2026 |
| Establish category authority | Rank for 25 high-intent keywords in marketing analytics | Keywords in top 10 | 25 keywords | Q4 2026 |

## 3. Audience Segments

### Segment 1: The Solo Marketing Analyst
- **Who they are:** First analytics hire at a SaaS company (50-200 employees). 2-5 years experience. Reports to the VP of Marketing.
- **Pain points:** Drowning in dashboard requests; can't connect marketing spend to revenue; leadership wants "attribution" but the data is messy.
- **Content preferences:** Step-by-step tutorials, templates they can implement in an afternoon, SQL snippets. Prefers 1,500-2,500 word blog posts with code examples.
- **Where they hang out:** LinkedIn, dbt Community Slack, Locally Optimistic Slack, r/analytics.
- **Stage in journey:** Awareness and consideration — they know they need a better tool but haven't evaluated options.

### Segment 2: The VP of Marketing
- **Who they are:** Marketing leader at a Series A-C SaaS company. 10+ years experience. Manages a team of 5-15.
- **Pain points:** Board wants marketing ROI numbers they can't produce; current tooling is a patchwork of GA4, spreadsheets, and Looker; can't justify headcount without proving impact.
- **Content preferences:** Executive summaries, benchmark reports, case studies. Prefers shorter formats (800-1,200 words) or 10-minute videos. Shares content that makes them look smart to their CEO.
- **Where they hang out:** LinkedIn, Pavilion community, SaaStr events, Lenny's Newsletter comments.
- **Stage in journey:** Consideration and decision — they're evaluating tools and need proof of value.

### Segment 3: The Growth Marketer
- **Who they are:** Growth or demand gen marketer running experiments across paid and organic channels. 3-7 years experience. Hands-on with tools daily.
- **Pain points:** Multi-touch attribution is broken in their current setup; can't tell which content actually drives pipeline; spending time building reports instead of optimizing campaigns.
- **Content preferences:** Framework breakdowns, comparison posts, "how we did it" case studies. Willing to read long-form (2,000-4,000 words) if it's actionable.
- **Where they hang out:** Twitter/X, Demand Curve community, podcasts (Lenny's, Marketing Against the Grain).
- **Stage in journey:** Awareness — they're searching for solutions to specific problems and might discover us through content.

## 4. Content Pillars

| Analytics Operations | Setting up analytics infrastructure, data hygiene, dashboard design, SQL patterns | Solo Analyst | Establish category authority | 25% |
| Executive Reporting | Communicating marketing impact to leadership, board-ready metrics, benchmarks | VP of Marketing | Generate qualified pipeline | 20% |
| Content & Campaign Measurement | Measuring content ROI, campaign performance analysis, experimentation frameworks | Growth Marketer | Reduce CAC (by attracting organic signups) | 20% |

## 8. Measurement Plan

### Leading Indicators (Weekly)
- Blog sessions from organic search: Are we growing organic discoverability?
- LinkedIn impressions and engagement rate: Is our content resonating with the right audience?
- Newsletter open rate (target: 42%+) and click rate (target: 8%+): Is our subscriber base engaged?
- Free trial signups from content (UTM-tagged): Is content driving conversion?

### Lagging Indicators (Monthly)
- Content-sourced MQLs: How many qualified leads originated from content?
- Keyword rankings (top 10 positions): Are we building search authority?
- Organic traffic as % of total: Is content reducing our dependence on paid acquisition?
- Pipeline influenced by content: How much revenue did content touch before close?

### Content Scorecard

| Metric | Current (March 2026) | Target (Q4 2026) | Tracking Tool |
|--------|---------------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Monthly organic sessions | 28,000 | 75,000 | Google Analytics 4 |
| Content-sourced MQLs/mo | 45 | 150 | HubSpot |
| Newsletter subscribers | 4,200 | 12,000 | ConvertKit |
| Keywords in top 10 | 8 | 25 | Ahrefs |
| Organic % of signups | 18% | 35% | Beamline internal |

## 9. Editorial Guidelines

### Voice & Tone
Clear, precise, and confident — like a smart colleague explaining something at a whiteboard. We use data in every piece (not just opinions). We're specific where others are vague. See the full Beamline Voice Guide for detailed examples.

### Quality Standards
- Every claim backed by a source, a data point, or an original analysis
- Code examples must be tested and runnable
- Screenshots must be current (within the last quarter)
- Minimum 2 internal links per blog post
- Every post includes a clear next step (CTA to free trial, related post, or template download)

### SEO Standards
- Primary keyword in title, H1, first 100 words, and meta description
- One primary keyword and 2-3 secondary keywords per post
- Meta descriptions: 145-160 characters, include primary keyword, end with a value proposition
- Title tags: Primary keyword near the front, under 60 characters
- Internal links: minimum 2 per post, using descriptive anchor text (not "click here")

### Approval Process
1. Writer submits draft in Google Docs with target keyword and audience segment noted
2. Editor reviews for quality standards, voice, and SEO within 2 business days
3. Author revises and resubmits
4. Editor approves or requests final changes
5. Published by content manager with proper UTM tagging and social promotion queued

## 10. Content Repurposing Framework

| Source Content | Derivative 1 | Derivative 2 | Derivative 3 | Derivative 4 |
|---------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Long-form tutorial (blog) | LinkedIn carousel (key steps) | Newsletter feature (summary + link) | Video walkthrough (YouTube) | 3 tweet-length tips (Twitter/X) |
| Benchmark report | 10 stat-based LinkedIn posts | Newsletter deep-dive series (3 parts) | PR pitch to industry publications | Infographic for social/embeds |
| Case study | LinkedIn story post | Sales one-pager (PDF) | Newsletter customer spotlight | Quote card for social |
| Video tutorial | Blog post (written version) | 3 short clips (social) | GIF walkthrough (help docs) | Newsletter embed |
```

## Customization

Replace all `[bracketed]` sections with your brand's specifics. Adjust the number of audience segments (2-4 is the range), content pillars (3-5), and channels (3-6) based on your team size and resources. A solo marketer should focus on 2-3 channels and 3 pillars. A team of 5+ can handle the full framework. The content cadence should reflect what you can actually sustain — overpromising and underdelivering is worse than starting small and scaling up.
