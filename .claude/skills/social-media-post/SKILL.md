# Social Media Post

Platform-native social media copy optimized for engagement on Twitter/X, LinkedIn, and Instagram. The marketer uses this when creating individual posts, campaign series, or a content calendar that needs to perform across platforms while respecting each platform's culture, format constraints, and algorithm preferences.

## Before you start

- **Product context.** This framework needs to know the product: name, what it does, who it serves. Take it from the request, the project's CLAUDE.md or README, or earlier conversation. If it is missing and matters for the output, ask one short question instead of inventing facts.
- **Language.** The framework is written in English; write the deliverable in the language of the request (Russian request → Russian output), and adapt examples, platforms and conventions to that market.
- **No invented numbers.** Placeholders in `[brackets]` are to be filled with real data from the user. Where data is missing, leave a clearly marked placeholder rather than fabricating metrics, quotes or customer names.

## How to Write Each Section

**Content Strategy:** Define the objective before writing a word. An awareness post is written differently than a conversion post. The key message forces you to distill what you are actually saying. If you cannot state the message in one sentence, the post is unfocused.

Good: "Key message: Engineering teams that automate status reports ship 30% faster because managers spend time on decisions instead of information gathering."

Bad: "Key message: Our product is great for engineering teams."

**Twitter/X Post:** The first line is everything. Twitter users scroll fast — you have about 1.5 seconds to earn a stop. The hook must be surprising, specific, or contrarian. No hashtags in the body text on X — they look spammy and reduce clicks. If you include a link, know that X suppresses link posts in the algorithm. Consider putting the link in a reply instead.

Good hook: "We cancelled 3 status meetings last month. Nobody noticed."
Bad hook: "Excited to share some thoughts on project management! #ProjectManagement #SaaS"

**LinkedIn Post:** The first 2 lines appear before the "see more" fold. These lines determine whether anyone reads the rest. Use line breaks aggressively — LinkedIn's mobile layout punishes dense paragraphs. Write in first person. LinkedIn rewards personal stories and opinions more than branded corporate content.

Good opening: "I spent 6 hours last week writing status updates.\n\nNone of them changed a single decision."
Bad opening: "We are thrilled to announce that our company has released an exciting new feature that we think you will love!"

**Instagram Post:** The visual does the work on Instagram. The caption supports the visual, not the other way around. For carousels, design the first slide as a hook — it must stop the scroll. Hashtags go in the first comment or at the very end of the caption, separated by line breaks.

**Hashtag Strategy:** Hashtags serve different purposes on each platform. On Twitter/X, use 0-2 hashtags and only for discoverability in specific communities. On LinkedIn, 3-5 hashtags at the end of the post. On Instagram, use 15-25 hashtags with a mix of broad (reach), medium (relevance), and niche (community).

## Conventions

- Every post has one key message. If you are saying two things, write two posts.
- Twitter/X posts: 0-2 hashtags, no links in the main post body (algorithm penalty), hook in the first line.
- LinkedIn posts: First 2 lines are the hook (pre-fold). Short paragraphs (1-2 sentences each). 3-5 hashtags at the end.
- Instagram captions: Hook in the first line. Visual carries the message. Hashtags in the first comment or after a line-break buffer.
- Platform-native writing means rewriting for each platform, not copying the same text to all three. A tweet is not a LinkedIn post.
- Posting times follow platform data: Twitter/X peaks 8-10am and 12-1pm. LinkedIn peaks 7-9am Tuesday-Thursday. Instagram peaks 11am-1pm and 7-9pm.
- Images should be native aspect ratios: Twitter/X 16:9, LinkedIn 1.91:1 or 1:1, Instagram 1:1 or 4:5.
- Every piece of media has alt text for accessibility.
- Threads on Twitter/X should be 4-8 tweets. Under 4 is not a thread. Over 8 loses readers.
- Reply to comments within 2 hours of posting for maximum algorithmic boost. Engagement in the first hour matters most.
- Tag people and companies sparingly and only when genuinely relevant. Tagging for attention without adding value is spam.

## Anti-Patterns

- Do NOT copy-paste the same text across all platforms. Each platform has its own format, culture, and character limits. A LinkedIn essay does not belong on Twitter.
- Do NOT use more than 2 hashtags on Twitter/X. It looks desperate and reduces engagement.
- Do NOT start LinkedIn posts with "Excited to announce" or "Thrilled to share." These openings are so overused they have become invisible. Start with a surprising statement, a number, or a question.
- Do NOT put links in the main body of a Twitter/X post. The algorithm suppresses posts with links. Put the link in the first reply.
- Do NOT write Instagram captions without considering the visual first. The image or carousel is the primary content — the caption supports it.
- Do NOT use hashtags inline on any platform. #Writing #Like #This #Makes #You #Look #Like #A #Bot. Hashtags go at the end or in the first comment.
- Do NOT post at random times. Check your platform analytics for peak engagement windows and schedule accordingly.
- Do NOT ignore comments on your posts. Replying to comments boosts visibility and builds community. Not replying signals that you are broadcasting, not engaging.
- Do NOT use stock photos for social media. Original screenshots, behind-the-scenes photos, and designed graphics outperform generic stock imagery.
- Do NOT write threads without a strong hook tweet. If Tweet 1 does not stop the scroll, nobody reads Tweets 2-8.
- Do NOT tag 10 people in every post hoping they will reshare. Tag people when the content is directly relevant to them. Anything else is spam.
- Do NOT post the same content format every day. Rotate between single posts, threads, carousels, polls, and stories to avoid content fatigue.
- Do NOT skip alt text on images. It is an accessibility requirement and an SEO signal on platforms that support it.
- Do NOT write Instagram carousel slides with walls of text. Each slide should have 1 key point in large, readable type. If a slide has more than 30 words, it is too dense.

## Customization

For single posts (not campaigns), strip down to just the platform sections you need. For content calendars, expand the Content Calendar Context section into a full weekly or monthly plan with themes per week. For community-driven brands, add a Community Engagement section with specific accounts to interact with, subreddits to post in, and Discord/Slack communities to share with. For B2C brands, shift more weight to Instagram and add TikTok/Reels scripts with trending audio recommendations.
