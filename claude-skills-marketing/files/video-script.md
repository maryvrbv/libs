### How to Write Each Section

**Video Strategy:** Define the objective and key message before scripting a single word. Every video must have one key message. If you cannot state it in one sentence, the video is unfocused and the viewer will leave.

Good: "Key message: You can cut your API testing time by 80% by auto-generating test suites from your OpenAPI spec using Parsely CLI."

Bad: "Key message: Parsely CLI is a great tool with lots of features."

**Thumbnail & Title:** The thumbnail and title are the ad for the video. They must work together — not repeat each other. The thumbnail creates curiosity visually (expression, contrast, text). The title creates curiosity verbally (keyword, number, outcome). Together, they earn the click.

Good title: "I Automated My Entire API Test Suite in 30 Seconds"
Good thumbnail: Shocked face + terminal screenshot + text overlay "30 SECONDS"

Bad title: "Parsely CLI v2.0 Product Overview and Feature Walkthrough"
Bad thumbnail: Company logo on a blue background

**Hook (first 5-10 seconds):** The hook determines whether the viewer stays or leaves. On YouTube, 20% of viewers leave in the first 5 seconds. The hook must be the most interesting part of the video, not the introduction. Start with a demonstration, a bold claim, or a problem statement — never "Hey guys, welcome to my channel."

Good hook: "I'm going to show you something that took me 3 weeks to do manually — and now takes 30 seconds." [shows terminal running the command]

Bad hook: "Hey everyone, welcome back to the channel. Today we're going to be talking about API testing. Before we get started, make sure to like and subscribe."

**Section Content:** Each section should teach one thing or advance the narrative by one step. Use the visual column to plan what the viewer sees — talking head, screen recording, B-roll, graphics. The audio column is the exact script. Write it conversationally, the way you speak, not the way you write.

**CTA Section:** The CTA must feel like a natural next step, not a sales pitch interruption. Transition from the content into the CTA: "If you want to try this yourself..." or "The tool I used for all of this is..." Never cut from educational content to "BUY MY PRODUCT" without a bridge.

### Conventions

- Hook is 5-10 seconds maximum. No introductions, no greetings, no channel bumpers before the hook.
- Each section teaches one concept or shows one step. If a section covers two topics, split it into two sections.
- The visual column is as important as the audio column. Viewers watch the screen — plan what they see at every moment.
- Talking head segments are under 30 seconds before a visual change (B-roll, screen recording, graphic). Static talking head for minutes causes viewer drop-off.
- Script is written in spoken language, not written language. Read it aloud. If it sounds like a blog post, rewrite it.
- Title includes the primary keyword in the first 50 characters. YouTube truncates titles after 60 characters on mobile.
- Description first 2 lines include the key value prop and main link. Everything after "Show more" is supplementary.
- Chapters/timestamps are included for any video over 5 minutes. They improve watch time and viewer experience.
- Thumbnail text is 2-5 words maximum at a size readable on a phone screen. If you cannot read the text at 120px wide, it is too small or too much.
- Short-form cuts are planned during scripting, not after editing. Identify the best 30-60 second moments while writing.
- B-roll notes are specific enough for a videographer to shoot without additional briefing.
- End screens recommend 2 videos: one recent upload and one high-performing evergreen video.

### Anti-Patterns

- Do NOT start with a channel introduction or greeting. "Hey guys, welcome back" costs you 20% of viewers before you say anything valuable. Start with the hook.
- Do NOT script in written language. "Furthermore, it should be noted that the implementation process is straightforward" is not how anyone speaks. "Setup takes about 2 minutes — let me show you" is.
- Do NOT plan only the audio. A script without visual direction is a podcast. Plan what the viewer sees at every timestamp.
- Do NOT make the CTA longer than 15 seconds. The viewer gave you their time for the content. Respect that by keeping the ask brief and clear.
- Do NOT use the same thumbnail style as every other creator in your niche. If every API testing thumbnail is a terminal screenshot, make yours a face + result.
- Do NOT skip the hook to "set context." Context is Section 1. The hook is the reason they stay for context.
- Do NOT show static slides for more than 15 seconds. If a concept needs a slide, animate it or break it into build steps.
- Do NOT ask viewers to subscribe before you have delivered any value. Earn the subscribe ask by proving the video is worth watching.
- Do NOT include a 30-second sponsor read in the first 2 minutes. Sponsor reads in the first 2 minutes spike audience drop-off. Place them after the first value delivery (usually 3-5 minutes in).
- Do NOT forget to plan short-form cuts during scripting. The best shorts come from moments designed to stand alone, not randomly clipped from a long video.
- Do NOT write 10-minute scripts for 5-minute topics. If the content is 5 minutes of information, the video is 5-7 minutes. Padding with filler increases average view duration percentage but decreases total viewer satisfaction.
- Do NOT ignore the description for SEO. YouTube's algorithm reads the description. Include your primary keyword naturally in the first 2 lines.
- Do NOT film without a teleprompter or detailed notes. Winging it leads to rambling, which leads to longer edits and higher viewer drop-off.
- Do NOT end the video without pointing to the next video. End screens and verbal recommendations keep viewers on your channel.

### Customization

For short-form only (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), strip the script down to Hook + 1 Section + CTA, all within 30-60 seconds. Use the Short-Form Cuts section as the primary script and skip the full-length sections. For webinar or long-form content (20-45 minutes), expand each section to 5-8 minutes with more depth, audience interaction prompts, and a Q&A section. For customer testimonial videos, replace the tutorial sections with Interview Question, Customer Answer (verbatim), and B-Roll Direction columns. For animated explainer videos (no talking head), remove the talking head visual references and expand the graphic/animation column with detailed motion design direction.
