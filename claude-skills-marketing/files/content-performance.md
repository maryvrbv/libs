## Skill: Content Performance

Content performance analysis is the process of measuring how content marketing assets — blog posts, guides, videos, case studies, landing pages — perform against engagement, conversion, and revenue goals. A marketer does this when auditing the content program, justifying content investment, identifying what topics and formats to double down on, and finding gaps in the content strategy.

### How to Write Each Section

**Content Program Overview**
This is the executive summary. Seven numbers that answer: how much content did we produce, how much traffic and conversions did it drive, how much did it cost, and was it worth it? Content ROI is the number leadership cares about most — but it must be calculated over the right time horizon. A blog post published today may not generate meaningful traffic for 3-6 months. Report both the period ROI (current quarter) and the trailing 12-month ROI to show the compounding effect.

Good: "Q1 2026: 24 content assets published. 142,000 organic sessions from content pages (+18% vs Q4 2025). 1,840 conversions (1.3% conversion rate). $124,600 revenue attributed. Content production cost: $38,400. Q1 ROI: 224%. Trailing 12-month ROI: 680% (reflecting traffic compounding from older content)."
Bad: "We published 24 pieces of content this quarter."

**Performance by Content Type**
Not all content types perform the same way. Blog posts typically drive the most traffic but convert at lower rates. Case studies and comparison pages drive less traffic but convert at higher rates. Gated content (ebooks, guides) generates leads directly. Understanding these patterns lets you allocate production resources to the type with the highest return for your goals.

Good: "Blog posts: 18 published, 98,000 sessions, 0.8% conversion rate, $42,000 revenue. Case studies: 3 published, 8,400 sessions, 4.2% conversion rate, $38,000 revenue. Case studies generate 5.25x more revenue per session than blog posts despite 11.7x fewer sessions."
Bad: "Blog posts got the most traffic."

**Performance by Topic**
Which topics resonate with your audience and convert? Group content by topic cluster or category. The topic with the highest conversion rate is your money topic — produce more of it. The topic with high traffic but low conversion may need better CTAs or be attracting the wrong audience.

Good: "Topic: 'Onboarding automation' — 4 posts, 24,000 sessions, 2.1% conversion rate, $52,000 revenue. This is our highest-converting topic cluster. Topic: 'HR industry news' — 6 posts, 32,000 sessions, 0.2% conversion rate, $4,200 revenue. High traffic, negligible conversions. This content attracts readers who are not in our ICP."
Bad: A topic breakdown with no conversion data — just traffic.

**SEO Performance**
Content marketing and SEO are deeply intertwined. Track organic sessions, ranking keyword counts (top 3, top 10), average position, organic CTR, and backlinks acquired. The keyword gap analysis identifies where competitors rank and you do not — these are content creation opportunities.

Good: "Top 10 ranking keywords: 342 (+28 vs last quarter). Top 3 ranking keywords: 86 (+12). The top-performing content piece, 'Complete Guide to Employee Onboarding,' ranks #2 for 'employee onboarding guide' (2,400 monthly searches) and generates 4,200 organic sessions/month."
Bad: "Our SEO is improving."

**Content Gap Analysis**
Identify four types of gaps: topic gaps (audience demand with no content), funnel gaps (stages of the buyer journey with insufficient content), format gaps (formats the audience engages with but you have not produced), and competitor gaps (topics where competitors rank and you do not). Each gap is a prioritized opportunity.

Good: "Funnel gap: We have 18 top-of-funnel blog posts but only 2 bottom-of-funnel comparison pages. Comparison pages convert at 6.8% — 8.5x higher than blog posts. Creating 5 comparison pages targeting our top competitor keywords could generate an estimated 120 additional conversions per quarter."
Bad: "We should create more content."

**Content ROI Analysis**
Break down the full cost of content — not just writer fees, but design, editing, SEO tools, distribution, and the portion of team salaries dedicated to content. Compare this to attributed revenue. The payback period is critical: how many months until a piece of content generates more revenue than it cost to produce? For evergreen content, this number improves over time as the content continues generating traffic without additional cost.

Good: "Total content investment Q1: $38,400. Revenue attributed (same period): $124,600. Q1 ROI: 224%. But our top blog post from Q2 2025 generated $18,400 in Q1 2026 at zero additional cost — the trailing ROI of content assets improves every quarter they remain relevant."
Bad: "Content ROI is positive."

### Conventions

- Always include both traffic AND conversion metrics. Traffic without conversions is vanity. Conversions without traffic context miss the funnel picture.
- Calculate content ROI over at least a 6-month horizon. Single-quarter ROI undervalues content because blog posts compound traffic over time.
- Separate performance by content type, topic, and channel. Aggregates hide the most actionable insights.
- Include an underperforming content section. Identifying what to stop or fix is as valuable as identifying what to create.
- SEO metrics must include keyword rankings, not just traffic. Rankings are the leading indicator; traffic is lagging.
- Content gap analysis must include all four gap types: topic, funnel, format, and competitor.
- Format: commas for thousands, one decimal place for percentages, dollar signs for revenue.
- Include publish date for every content asset. Content performance changes dramatically with age — a 3-month-old post and a 3-year-old post are in different lifecycle stages.
- Always note the attribution model and window used for revenue attribution. "Content generated $124K" means different things under first-touch vs last-touch.
- The audience is the content team, the broader marketing team, and leadership who allocate headcount and budget.
- Include trend vs previous period for all key metrics. Absolute numbers without direction are not actionable.
- When reporting organic traffic, distinguish between branded and non-branded search. Branded organic is demand capture; non-branded organic is demand generation.

### Anti-Patterns

- Do NOT measure content performance by traffic alone. A blog post with 50,000 sessions and zero conversions is not a high-performing piece — it is attracting the wrong audience or missing a CTA.
- Do NOT calculate content ROI using a single month. Content is a compounding asset. A blog post that costs $800 to produce and generates $200 in month one may generate $5,000 over 12 months.
- Do NOT evaluate all content types by the same metric. Blog posts should be measured on organic traffic and assisted conversions. Case studies should be measured on conversion rate. Gated content should be measured on lead generation. Comparing a blog post's conversion rate to a landing page's conversion rate is misleading.
- Do NOT ignore underperforming content. The bottom 20% of your content library may be dragging down site-wide SEO quality. Audit, consolidate, redirect, or remove low-value pages.
- Do NOT treat social shares as a primary success metric. Social engagement rarely correlates with conversions. A viral post that generates zero leads is not a business success.
- Do NOT skip the content gap analysis. Creating more content in topics you already cover well while ignoring gaps in your funnel or keyword strategy is wasted effort.
- Do NOT compare content performance across different time horizons without normalizing. A post published 12 months ago has had far more time to accumulate traffic than one published last week.
- Do NOT attribute all revenue from a page to the content itself. If 80% of a page's traffic comes from paid ads, the content deserves partial credit — not full credit.
- Do NOT ignore the cost side of content ROI. "Our blog generated $200K in revenue" is incomplete without knowing the $60K it cost to produce.
- Do NOT measure video content by views alone. Views without watch time, completion rate, and downstream conversion data are vanity metrics.
- Do NOT forget to measure content's role in the full funnel. Top-of-funnel blog posts may not directly convert, but they may be the first touch in a multi-touch conversion path. Use assisted conversion reporting.
- Do NOT create a content performance report without a recommendation section. The analysis exists to inform decisions — what to create next, what to optimize, what to retire.
- Do NOT aggregate all organic traffic into one number. Separate content page traffic from product page traffic, homepage traffic, and support/docs traffic.
- Do NOT assume a declining content piece is a failure. Seasonal content, trend-based content, and news content naturally decay. Evergreen content that declines may need updating.

**Key type insights:**
- Case studies have the highest ROI (767%) despite the fewest sessions. They are bottom-of-funnel assets that convert prospects who are already evaluating solutions. The 3.22% conversion rate is 4x the blog post rate.

- Blog posts drive the most traffic but convert at the lowest rate (0.80%). Their value is primarily in first-touch attribution — they start the buyer journey.
- Webinar recordings have the lowest ROI (107%) due to high production costs ($4,500/webinar). However, they have the highest engagement depth (18:42 avg watch time) and attendees who convert become high-ACV customers.

### Customization

Replace all bracket sections with your specifics. For e-commerce content, replace MQLs with product page views, add-to-carts, and purchases as conversion events. For media companies, replace revenue with engagement metrics (time on site, pages per session, subscriber conversions). For B2C brands, add social performance (shares, saves, UGC generated) alongside SEO and conversion metrics. For video-first content programs, track watch time, completion rate, and subscriber growth in addition to views. For companies with a mature content library (200+ assets), add a content refresh section tracking which older assets were updated and the performance impact of the refresh. For marketplace businesses, create separate content performance analyses for supply-side and demand-side content.
