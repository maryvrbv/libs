## Skill: Partnership Marketing

A partnership marketing strategy defines how you collaborate with other companies to reach new audiences, create joint value, and grow faster than either partner could alone. Marketers write this to systematize partner identification, outreach, campaign execution, and revenue sharing. It answers: "Who should we partner with, how should we structure the deal, and how do we make it work for both sides?"

### How to Write Each Section

**Partnership Strategy Overview**
Start with why partnerships matter for your specific business. Are you trying to reach an audience you can't access through paid channels? Build credibility through association? Add product value through integrations? The "why" determines which partnership types to prioritize. A startup with a small audience benefits most from co-marketing swaps. A platform with an API benefits most from integration partnerships.

Good: "We can't efficiently reach analytics managers through paid channels — CPL on LinkedIn exceeds $45. Three integration partners (Segment, Mixpanel, Looker) have 10K+ analytics managers in their user bases who already need a tool like ours. Integration partnerships give us distribution through product value, not advertising."

Bad: "Partnerships are important for growth." (No specificity, no rationale, no prioritization.)

**Partner Identification**
Be selective. Five strong partnerships outperform fifty weak ones. Score every potential partner against your criteria before investing outreach effort. Tier them — Tier 1 gets dedicated attention, Tier 3 gets templated outreach and opportunistic collaboration. The most important criterion is audience overlap — a partner with 100K followers who aren't your ICP is less valuable than a partner with 5K followers who are.

**Outreach Strategy**
Cold outreach to potential partners has a 5-15% response rate. Warm intros through mutual connections, events, or genuine social engagement before the ask increase response rates to 30-50%. The first message should be specific (not "let's explore synergies"), short (under 150 words), and lead with what you bring to them, not what you want from them.

**Joint Campaign Planning**
The most common reason partnerships fail is misaligned expectations. Write a campaign brief for every joint initiative — even small ones. Define who does what, by when, and how leads are handled. The lead handling section is critical — if both companies email the same lead with competing offers, the experience is terrible for the recipient and the partnership.

**Revenue Sharing**
Keep the structure simple. If the partner sources the lead, they get the larger share. If you source the lead, you get the larger share. Overcomplicated revenue sharing creates accounting headaches and disputes. For affiliate partnerships, recurring commissions (% of monthly revenue for the customer lifetime) incentivize partners to send high-quality leads, not just volume.

### Conventions

- Length: 5-8 pages
- Tone: Strategic, relationship-focused, but backed by metrics
- Prioritize depth of partnership over breadth — 5 great partners beat 20 mediocre ones
- Always lead outreach with what you bring to the partner, not what you want from them
- Write a campaign brief for every joint initiative — even small content swaps
- Define lead handling before launching any co-marketing campaign
- Review each partnership quarterly — don't let underperforming partnerships linger
- Keep revenue sharing structures simple — complex splits create disputes
- Get legal agreements in place before sharing data or co-branding
- Track partner-sourced leads separately in your CRM
- Treat partners like customers — their satisfaction determines partnership longevity
- Document everything — who said what, who committed to what, what the results were

### Anti-Patterns

- Do NOT pursue partnerships without a clear mutual benefit — one-sided deals don't last
- Do NOT approach partners with "let's explore synergies" — vague pitches get ignored. Propose something specific
- Do NOT skip the campaign brief for "small" partnerships — misaligned expectations ruin relationships
- Do NOT share customer data without a legal agreement — this violates trust and potentially law
- Do NOT measure partnerships only by leads generated — brand association, backlinks, and audience access have value that doesn't show up in lead counts
- Do NOT treat partnership outreach like cold sales — you're proposing a relationship, not closing a transaction
- Do NOT partner with companies whose brand or quality standards are significantly lower than yours — the association hurts you more than the distribution helps
- Do NOT commit to exclusivity unless the partner's contribution is extraordinary — exclusivity limits your options for minimal gain in most cases
- Do NOT launch integration partnerships without maintenance plans — a broken integration is worse than no integration
- Do NOT ignore partner feedback — if they say the campaign underperformed on their end, take it seriously and adjust
- Do NOT run joint campaigns without UTM tracking — if you can't attribute results, you can't prove value to either side
- Do NOT treat affiliate and co-marketing partnerships the same — they have different incentive structures, management overhead, and measurement approaches
- Do NOT let partnerships die from neglect — schedule recurring check-ins, even when there's no active campaign

### Customization

Replace all `[bracketed]` sections with your actual partner data, metrics, and commercial terms. Adjust the number of partners based on your team's capacity — 1 person can effectively manage 3-5 active partnerships. Scale the evaluation criteria weights to reflect your strategic priorities — if integrations are your primary partnership type, weight product complementarity higher. Outreach templates should be customized per partner with specific, researched details — never send the same template to multiple partners.
