## Skill: Campaign Brief

A campaign brief is the planning document for a coordinated marketing effort. Marketers write this before launching any campaign — product launches, seasonal promotions, brand awareness pushes, or demand gen programs. It aligns creative, channel, and measurement teams on what the campaign needs to achieve, who it targets, and how success will be measured. Without it, campaigns drift, budgets waste, and teams pull in different directions.

### How to Write Each Section

**Campaign Objective**
One sentence. One primary objective. If you have two equally important objectives, you have zero — because the team will optimize for whichever is easier to hit. The primary objective should be measurable and time-bound. "Generate 500 marketing-qualified leads in 6 weeks" is an objective. "Increase brand awareness" is a wish.

Good: "Generate 200 free trial signups from mid-market SaaS companies within 4 weeks of launch, at a cost per trial under $85."

Bad: "Drive awareness and consideration for our new product." (Not measurable, not specific.)

**Background & Context**
Explain why this campaign exists right now. What changed? Did you launch something? Did a competitor make a move? Is there a seasonal window? Include learnings from past campaigns that inform decisions in this brief. This section gives context to everyone who reads the brief — especially the people creating assets who weren't in the planning meeting.

**Target Audience**
Be specific enough that a media buyer can target this audience and a copywriter can write to them. Include where to reach them — channels, publications, communities. The exclusions section is equally important: who are you NOT targeting? This prevents wasted spend and off-message creative.

**Key Message**
The one-liner is the single thing the audience should remember. If they forget everything else, this sticks. Supporting messages add depth and proof. The CTA is the specific action — not "learn more" (too vague) but "start a free 14-day trial" (specific and measurable).

**Channel Plan**
Map every channel to a funnel stage. Paid social might drive awareness, retargeting drives consideration, email drives conversion. Each channel needs a clear role, defined content requirements, a budget allocation, and an owner. A channel without an owner is a channel that underperforms.

**Budget**
Break budget into categories so the team knows what's allocated to paid media vs. creative production vs. tools. Include a 10-15% contingency for opportunities or pivots that emerge during the campaign. If budget is unknown, set ranges and note what needs approval.

**Success Metrics**
Define 3-5 metrics maximum. One primary metric that defines whether the campaign succeeded. Two to three secondary metrics that provide context. One efficiency metric (cost per lead, ROAS, cost per trial). Include the measurement tool and reporting cadence for each.

### Conventions

- Length: 2-4 pages
- Tone: Clear and directive. This is a coordination document — everyone reading it should know exactly what they need to do
- One primary objective per campaign. Supporting objectives are fine, but the primary must be unambiguous
- Every channel must have an owner. Channels without owners get neglected
- Budget should always include a 10-15% contingency
- Include the reporting schedule — daily pacing checks, weekly optimization reviews, post-campaign retro
- Creative requirements should include specs (dimensions, file formats, character limits) not just descriptions
- Timeline should map to phases, not individual tasks — this is a brief, not a project plan
- Include audience exclusions — knowing who you're NOT targeting prevents wasted spend
- Link to supporting docs (brand guidelines, audience research, competitive analysis) rather than duplicating content
- Every metric needs a target number, a measurement tool, and a reporting cadence

### Anti-Patterns

- Do NOT launch a campaign without an approved brief — skipping the brief doesn't save time, it wastes budget
- Do NOT have more than one primary objective — "generate leads AND build brand awareness AND drive product adoption" means nobody knows what to optimize for
- Do NOT set targets without historical context — if you've never run LinkedIn ads before, don't set a target CPA without benchmarks
- Do NOT skip the creative requirements table — "we need some ads" is not a brief for a designer. Include specs, due dates, and owners
- Do NOT plan channels without defining each one's funnel role — running the same message at the same intensity on every channel wastes money
- Do NOT allocate 100% of budget to media without accounting for creative production — great targeting with bad creative burns money
- Do NOT forget audience exclusions — targeting "all marketers" when you sell to "enterprise B2B marketing leaders" wastes 80% of your impressions
- Do NOT set the campaign live without confirming tracking is working — UTM parameters, conversion pixels, and attribution tags should be verified before spend begins
- Do NOT plan a 6-month campaign as a single brief — campaigns longer than 8 weeks need phase-level briefs or they become unmanageable
- Do NOT omit the risks section — every campaign has failure modes. Naming them upfront lets you respond faster when something breaks
- Do NOT write a brief and skip the retrospective — campaigns you don't measure teach you nothing
- Do NOT optimize for clicks when the objective is conversions — misaligned optimization wastes budget on vanity metrics

# Campaign Brief: Meridian Analytics — Product Hunt Launch

**Author:** Sofia Reyes
**Last Updated:** 2026-03-25
**Campaign Type:** Product Launch
**Status:** Approved

## 1. Campaign Objective

**Primary Objective:** Achieve #1 Product of the Day on Product Hunt and generate 1,500 free trial signups within 7 days of launch, at a cost per trial under $12.

**Secondary Objective:** Add 3,000 subscribers to the Meridian email list for ongoing nurture.

## 2. Background & Context

Meridian Analytics has been in private beta for 6 months with 47 paying customers. NPS is 72, and 3 customers have provided written case studies. We've validated product-market fit in the B2B SaaS marketing analytics segment. Product Hunt is our launch channel because our primary audience (marketing leaders and marketing ops professionals) is active on the platform, and a strong PH launch provides a 2-4 week halo of organic traffic from press and social sharing. Our competitor HockeyStack launched on PH 18 months ago and got 600+ upvotes — we need to beat that to establish positioning in the market.

## 3. Target Audience

### Primary Audience
- **Who:** VP of Marketing or Director of Demand Gen at B2B SaaS companies (Series A-D, 50-500 employees)
- **Pain Point:** Can't connect marketing spend to pipeline and revenue in a way their CEO trusts
- **Current Behavior:** Using GA4 + Salesforce reports + spreadsheets to cobble together attribution. Spending 15+ hours/month on reporting
- **Where to Reach Them:** Product Hunt, LinkedIn, Twitter/X, SaaStr Slack, Pavilion community, Lenny's Newsletter audience

### Secondary Audience
- **Who:** Marketing Operations Managers and Marketing Analysts at the same company profile
- **Pain Point:** Building and maintaining custom attribution dashboards is eating all their time
- **Where to Reach Them:** Product Hunt, Twitter/X, dbt Slack, Locally Optimistic Slack, r/analytics

### Exclusions
- Enterprise companies (500+ employees) — our product isn't ready for enterprise scale
- Non-SaaS companies — our integrations and attribution models are SaaS-specific
- Agencies — our pricing model doesn't support multi-client setups yet

## 4. Key Message

**One-liner:** Meridian gives B2B marketing teams board-ready attribution reports in 14 days — no data engineering required.

**Supporting Messages:**
1. The only attribution platform with built-in incrementality testing — know what actually caused revenue, not just what correlated with it (94% of customers report increased confidence in ROI reporting)
2. Go live in 14 days, not 3 months — pre-built integrations with Salesforce, HubSpot, and 20+ ad platforms mean zero custom API work
3. Marketing leaders at Lattice, Clearbit, and 45 other B2B SaaS companies trust Meridian for board-level reporting

**CTA:** Start a free 14-day trial — your first attribution report is ready in hours, not weeks.

### Creative Direction
Clean, data-forward, confident. Use dark backgrounds with bright data visualization accents (our brand green #00D68F). Show the product in context — real dashboards, real data, real reports. Avoid generic stock photography of people pointing at screens.

### Messaging Do's and Don'ts
- **Do:** Lead with "14 days to first report" — speed is our sharpest differentiator
- **Do:** Use specific customer results ("Lattice reduced blended CAC by 22%")
- **Don't:** Say "AI-powered" as a headline — every competitor claims this. Mention AI only in context of incrementality testing
- **Don't:** Compare directly to Bizible in ad creative — we don't want to look like a budget alternative. Position as a modern replacement

### Customization

Replace all `[bracketed]` sections with your campaign specifics. Adjust the number of channels (3-6 is typical), creative assets, and timeline phases based on campaign scope. For smaller campaigns (single channel, limited budget), you can trim sections — but never cut the objective, audience, key message, success metrics, or timeline. Those five sections prevent more wasted spend than everything else combined. For campaigns over $50K, add a section for legal and compliance review.
