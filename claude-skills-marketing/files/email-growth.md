## Skill: Email List Growth

An email list growth strategy defines how you build, segment, and maintain a high-quality subscriber base. Marketers write this to turn anonymous traffic into addressable audience. It answers: "How do we grow a list of people who actually want to hear from us — and how do we keep it healthy?"

### How to Write Each Section

**Current State**
Audit your list before planning growth. Pull real numbers from your ESP. Segment your existing subscribers — most lists have a significant portion of inactive subscribers who suppress your metrics. Knowing your current engagement rates sets the baseline for measuring improvements.

Good: "Total subscribers: 8,400. Active (opened in 90 days): 4,200 (50%). Monthly growth rate: 3.2%. Average open rate: 28.4%. Spam complaint rate: 0.02%. Our blog subscription form drives 60% of new subscribers, but those subscribers have the lowest engagement (19% open rate vs. 38% for lead magnet downloaders)."

Bad: "We have about 8,000 subscribers." (No engagement breakdown, no source analysis, no actionable insight.)

**Acquisition Channels**
List every channel that contributes subscribers, with real numbers. Include cost per subscriber — even "free" channels like organic blog have an opportunity cost. Prioritize channels by quality (engagement of acquired subscribers), not just volume. 100 engaged subscribers from a webinar are worth more than 500 from a giveaway.

**Lead Magnet Strategy**
Every lead magnet should solve a specific, immediate problem for a specific persona. Generic content ("The Ultimate Guide to Marketing") attracts generic subscribers who don't engage. A lead magnet that takes 5 minutes to consume and solves one pain point converts better than a 50-page ebook. Track not just download rates but downstream engagement — does this lead magnet produce subscribers who open emails and buy?

**Segmentation Framework**
Segment by behavior, not just demographics. What someone does (clicks, downloads, visits, buys) is more predictive than who they are (job title, company size). Start with 4-5 segments. More than 8-10 creates operational complexity without proportional benefit. Every segment needs a different sending strategy — if you send the same email to every segment, you don't really have segments.

**List Hygiene**
A smaller, engaged list outperforms a larger, disengaged one. Clean your list quarterly at minimum. Run re-engagement campaigns before removing subscribers — some will come back. But if they don't respond to 3 win-back emails over 2 weeks, they're gone. Keeping them hurts your deliverability, which hurts everyone else on your list.

### Conventions

- Length: 4-7 pages
- Tone: Strategic and specific. Every tactic should have a measurable expected outcome
- Update list health metrics monthly, full strategy quarterly
- Always use double opt-in or email verification for new subscribers — it protects deliverability
- Track cost per subscriber by channel, including time investment for "free" channels
- Every lead magnet gets its own landing page — don't hide them behind generic forms
- Segment from day one — don't wait until the list is "big enough"
- Set net growth targets (new minus churn), not gross growth targets
- Include a re-engagement sequence before cleaning — give subscribers a chance to stay
- Monitor deliverability metrics (inbox placement, blacklists, domain reputation) weekly
- Never buy or rent email lists — the deliverability damage exceeds any short-term gain
- Tag subscribers by source and lead magnet from the moment they join — you can't retroactively segment

### Anti-Patterns

- Do NOT buy email lists — purchased lists have near-zero engagement and will get your domain blacklisted
- Do NOT add people to your list without explicit opt-in — this violates CAN-SPAM, GDPR, and destroys deliverability
- Do NOT grow the list without a plan to engage it — a list you don't email decays at 25-30% per year
- Do NOT send the same content to every subscriber — unsegmented blasts drive unsubscribes
- Do NOT ignore deliverability — if your emails land in spam, your list size is meaningless
- Do NOT use misleading subject lines to boost open rates — it trains subscribers not to trust you
- Do NOT skip the welcome sequence — the first 48 hours after opt-in have the highest engagement. Waste them and you lose the subscriber
- Do NOT create lead magnets that are thinly veiled sales pitches — if the lead magnet doesn't deliver standalone value, subscribers will disengage immediately
- Do NOT measure list growth by gross additions alone — if you add 500 and lose 400, your net growth is 100
- Do NOT keep unengaged subscribers indefinitely — they drag down your open rates, hurt sender reputation, and inflate your ESP costs
- Do NOT use single opt-in for paid acquisition channels — bot signups and typo emails will contaminate your list
- Do NOT email subscribers who haven't opened in 180+ days on your main list — move them to re-engagement or suppress them
- Do NOT change your sending domain or From address frequently — consistency builds sender reputation
- Do NOT optimize opt-in rate at the expense of subscriber quality — a popup that says "Win an iPad!" gets signups, not subscribers
