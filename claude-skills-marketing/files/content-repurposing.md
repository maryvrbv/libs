## Skill: Content Repurposing

Content repurposing is the process of transforming one piece of source content into multiple formats optimized for different platforms and audiences. A marketer uses this whenever a high-performing piece of content (webinar, blog post, podcast, research report) deserves broader distribution than a single format and channel can provide. The goal is to extract maximum value from every content investment.

### How to Write Each Section

**Source Content Summary**
Before you repurpose anything, distill the source material to its essence. The core message is one sentence — the single takeaway that survives across all formats. The key points are the 3-5 sub-arguments or sections that support the core message. The quotable stats are numbers or phrases that can stand alone as social posts, pull quotes, or email subject lines. This summary is the foundation — every derivative pulls from it.

Good core message: "Companies that connect customer feedback to revenue data build products that retain 40% better than those that rely on volume-based prioritization."
Bad core message: "Customer feedback is important for product development."

The core message should pass the "would someone share this?" test. If it is too generic to share, it is too generic to repurpose effectively.

**Format Adaptation Matrix**
List every possible derivative format, even ones you ultimately will not pursue. The matrix forces you to think about the full potential of the source content before you start producing. Score each derivative on audience fit (does this platform's audience care about this topic?), effort (how much work to produce?), and priority (P1 = do first, P2 = do if time allows, P3 = nice to have).

The priority should be driven by two factors: (1) where your audience actually spends time, and (2) which formats have the highest ROI based on past performance. If your LinkedIn posts consistently drive trial signups and your Twitter posts do not, LinkedIn derivatives are P1 regardless of how easy Twitter posts are to produce.

**Platform-Specific Adaptations**
This is where the real work happens. Each platform has different format constraints, audience expectations, and engagement patterns. A LinkedIn carousel is not a blog post with slides. A Twitter thread is not a blog post with line breaks. Each adaptation should feel native to its platform.

For each derivative, document:
- What specifically to create (not just "a LinkedIn post" but "a 7-slide carousel walking through the 5-step framework with a CTA on slide 7")
- How to modify the message (a technical blog post about API performance becomes a business-impact story on LinkedIn)
- Length and format requirements (Twitter: 280 chars per tweet, LinkedIn: ~3,000 chars before truncation, Instagram: 2,200 chars caption)
- The CTA for this specific platform (LinkedIn might drive to blog, Twitter might drive to newsletter, email might drive to webinar replay)

**Publishing Schedule**
Spread derivatives across 2-4 weeks. Do not publish everything on the same day. The source content publishes first, then derivatives drip out to maximize the content's lifespan. Front-load high-priority derivatives in week 1. Schedule evergreen derivatives (infographics, guides) for weeks 2-3. This creates sustained visibility rather than a single spike.

**Performance Tracking**
Define the primary metric for each derivative and set a target before publishing. Track actuals after publishing. This data informs future repurposing decisions — if LinkedIn carousels consistently outperform threads for your audience, prioritize carousels in future plans.

### Conventions

- Always start with the source content summary. If you cannot distill it to a core message and 5 key points, the source content may not be strong enough to repurpose.
- Prioritize derivatives by audience presence, not production ease. A 30-minute Twitter thread is worthless if your audience is on LinkedIn.
- Each derivative should feel native to its platform. Copy-pasting the same text across platforms is distribution, not repurposing.
- Spread publication across 2-4 weeks. A single content investment should have a 2-4 week lifespan, not a 1-day lifespan.
- Include the CTA for each derivative. Different platforms warrant different CTAs — social posts might link to the blog, the blog might link to a lead magnet, the email might link to a demo.
- Track performance by derivative, not just by source content. You need to know which formats and platforms drive results.
- Link derivatives back to the source when appropriate. Cross-linking builds SEO value and gives the audience a path to deeper content.
- Batch production by format type. Write all the social copy at once, design all the visual assets at once. Context-switching between formats is expensive.
- Assign owners and deadlines. Repurposing plans without accountability become idea lists.
- Reuse the same framework for every piece of source content. Consistency in process reduces production time over iterations.
- Update the performance tracking section 2 weeks after the last derivative publishes. Use this data to refine the next repurposing plan.
- Keep the adaptation matrix as a living reference. Over time, you will know which derivatives are worth the effort and which are not.

### Anti-Patterns

- Do NOT copy-paste the same text across platforms and call it repurposing. Each platform has different norms, formats, and audience expectations. Adapting is work — that is the point.
- Do NOT repurpose weak source content. If the original piece did not perform well or does not have a clear core message, repurposing amplifies the weakness, not the value.
- Do NOT create derivatives for platforms where your audience does not exist. Having a presence on 8 platforms is not a goal — reaching your audience where they spend time is.
- Do NOT publish all derivatives on the same day. Spread them across 2-4 weeks for sustained visibility.
- Do NOT skip the adaptation notes. "Make a LinkedIn post from this blog" without specifying how to adapt the message results in a post that feels like a copy-paste excerpt.
- Do NOT forget the CTA. Every derivative should have a clear next step for the audience, even if it is just "follow for more."
- Do NOT repurpose without tracking results. If you do not know which derivatives drive outcomes, you cannot improve the process.
- Do NOT treat repurposing as a one-time project. Build it into the content workflow — every major piece of content gets a repurposing plan as part of its production process.
- Do NOT force every source piece into 10 derivatives. Some content naturally yields 3-4 strong derivatives. Padding the plan with low-value derivatives wastes production time.
- Do NOT ignore format constraints. A 2,000-word blog post does not become a single Instagram caption. It becomes a carousel, a series of stories, or an infographic — each with its own structure.
- Do NOT skip the source content summary. Without a clear distillation, each derivative will emphasize different aspects of the source, creating an inconsistent message across platforms.
- Do NOT repurpose time-sensitive content into evergreen formats without updating. A "2025 Trends" webinar cannot become a "how to" blog post without removing the year-specific framing.

#### Content Repurposing Plan: FieldOps Webinar

**Product Context:** FieldOps is a field service management platform for HVAC, plumbing, and electrical companies. ## Source Content Summary

**Core Message:** HVAC companies that use route optimization and smart scheduling fill 30% more jobs per technician per day without adding headcount — the constraint is not technicians, it is scheduling inefficiency.

**Key Points:**
1. The average HVAC company loses 2.3 hours per technician per day to drive time between jobs — route optimization cuts this to 1.1 hours.
2. Manual scheduling creates 15-20% daily capacity waste because dispatchers cannot optimize in real time when cancellations, emergencies, or early completions happen.
3. Three case study companies (Comfort Air, PeakFlow, ThermalPro) increased jobs-per-tech by 28%, 31%, and 34% respectively within 90 days of implementing smart scheduling.
4. The ROI equation: if an average job generates $340 in revenue and you schedule 2 more jobs per tech per day with 5 technicians, that is $3,400/day = ~$850K/year in additional revenue capacity.
5. Implementation does not require ripping out existing tools — FieldOps connects to QuickBooks, ServiceTitan, and Housecall Pro.

**Data Points / Quotable Stats:**
- "HVAC companies lose 2.3 hours per technician per day to unnecessary drive time"
- "30% more jobs per tech without adding a single hire"
- "Comfort Air went from 4.2 to 5.4 jobs per tech per day in 90 days"
- "$850K/year in additional revenue capacity for a 5-tech company"
- "15-20% of daily scheduling capacity is wasted in manual dispatch"

**Target Audience:** HVAC company owners and operations managers with 3-20 technicians, currently using manual scheduling or basic tools.

## Platform-Specific Adaptations

### 1. Blog Post — Website/SEO

**What to create:** A 1,500-word blog post titled "How to Schedule 30% More HVAC Jobs Without Hiring (Data from 3 Companies)" targeting the keyword cluster: "HVAC scheduling optimization," "how to schedule more service calls," "field service route optimization."

**Adaptation notes:**
- Restructure from presentation narrative to scannable blog format — headers, bullet points, pull quotes
- Add an SEO introduction that targets the primary keyword
- Include the 3 case studies as proof sections with specific numbers
- Add a CTA at the bottom: free scheduling efficiency audit
- Include the ROI calculator as an interactive element or embedded spreadsheet

**Content outline:**
1. The scheduling problem: 2.3 hours lost per tech per day
2. Why manual dispatch creates 15-20% waste
3. Case study 1: Comfort Air — 28% increase
4. Case study 2: PeakFlow — 31% increase
5. Case study 3: ThermalPro — 34% increase
6. The ROI math: $850K for a 5-tech company
7. How to get started (CTA: free audit)

**Production requirements:**
- Writing: 3-4 hours (repurposing webinar transcript + adding SEO structure)
- Design: 1-2 hours (charts, pull quote graphics, case study callout boxes)
- SEO: 1 hour (keyword research, meta description, internal linking)

---

### 2. Email to Non-Attendees — Email

**What to create:** A single email to the 170 registrants who did not attend, summarizing the top 3 takeaways with a link to the recording and a CTA for a free scheduling audit.

**Adaptation notes:**
- Subject line should lead with the most compelling stat: "2.3 hours per tech per day — lost to drive time"
- Keep body under 250 words — this is a nudge, not a recap
- Include 3 bullet-point takeaways, each with a number
- Primary CTA: watch the replay. Secondary CTA: book a free audit

**Content outline:**
- Opening: "You registered for [webinar] but couldn't make it. Here's what you missed in 60 seconds."
- 3 key takeaways (one sentence each with stats)
- Link to replay
- P.S. line with audit CTA

**Production requirements:**
- Writing: 30 minutes
- Design: None (plain text email performs better for this audience)
- Send: Same day as webinar or next business day

---

### 3. LinkedIn Carousel + Article — LinkedIn

**What to create:** A 10-slide carousel walking through the "30% more jobs" framework with the 3 case studies, plus a shorter 500-word article for LinkedIn's native publishing.

**Adaptation notes:**

- Slide sequence: Hook (the 2.3 hours stat) → Problem → Solution framework → 3 case studies (1 slide each) → ROI math → CTA
- Article: conversational tone, first-person from the webinar presenter, link to full replay
- CTA: comment "SCHEDULE" to get the case study PDF (drives engagement + lead capture)

**Content outline (carousel):**
1. "HVAC companies lose 2.3 hours per tech per day to drive time" (hook)
2. "That's $850K/year in unrealized revenue for a 5-tech company" (stakes)
3. "The fix: route optimization + smart scheduling" (solution)
4. "Comfort Air: 4.2 → 5.4 jobs/tech/day in 90 days" (proof)
5. "PeakFlow: 31% more jobs, same team" (proof)
6. "ThermalPro: 34% increase, 90 days" (proof)
7. "The ROI equation" (math breakdown)
8. "You don't need to replace your tools" (objection handling)
9. "Comment SCHEDULE to get the full case study" (CTA)

**Production requirements:**
- Writing: 1.5 hours (carousel copy + article)
- Design: 2 hours (9 carousel slides in brand template)

### Customization

Replace the source content with your actual high-performing piece. Adjust the derivative formats to match the platforms where your audience is active. Remove platforms you do not use — a repurposing plan with 10 derivatives for a team of 1 is aspirational fiction. Start with 4-5 high-priority derivatives and add more as capacity allows.

Build a repurposing template specific to your recurring content types. If you host monthly webinars, the repurposing plan should be nearly identical each time — only the content changes. Systematize the process so it becomes a checklist, not a creative exercise.
