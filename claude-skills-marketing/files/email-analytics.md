## Skill: Email Analytics

Email analytics is the process of measuring email marketing performance across deliverability, engagement, list health, and revenue attribution — then using those measurements to improve future sends. A marketer does this when reviewing a monthly email program, diagnosing engagement drops, preparing for a list segmentation overhaul, or justifying email marketing investment to leadership.

### How to Write Each Section

**Email Program Overview**
This is the executive summary. Six numbers that tell the full story: how many emails went out, to how many people, how many times, and how much revenue it generated. Revenue per email sent is the efficiency metric that matters most to leadership — it answers "is email worth the investment?" directly.

Good: "February 2026: 124,000 emails sent to 28,400 active subscribers (4.4 sends/subscriber). Revenue attributed: $86,200. Revenue per email sent: $0.70."
Bad: "We sent a lot of emails this month and had good engagement."

**Deliverability Metrics**
Deliverability is the foundation. If emails are not reaching inboxes, nothing else matters. Track delivery rate, hard and soft bounce rates, spam complaint rate, and inbox placement rate (if you have a tool like GlockApps or Validity). Red flags: delivery rate below 97%, hard bounce rate above 0.5%, spam complaint rate above 0.1%. Any of these signals a list hygiene or sending reputation problem.

Good: "Delivery rate: 98.4% (on track). Hard bounce rate: 0.3% (on track). Spam complaint rate: 0.08% (on track). Inbox placement rate: 89% (Gmail: 92%, Outlook: 84%, Yahoo: 91%). Outlook placement dropped 6pp from last month — investigating potential blocklist issue."
Bad: "Deliverability looks fine."

**Engagement Metrics**
Open rate, click rate (CTR), and click-to-open rate (CTOR) are the three core engagement metrics. Open rate is directional (Apple MPP inflates it) but useful for trending over time if your methodology is consistent. CTR is the most reliable engagement metric. CTOR measures content quality — of the people who opened, how many clicked? This isolates content performance from subject line performance.

Industry benchmarks vary by sector:
- B2B SaaS: Open 20-28%, CTR 2-5%, CTOR 10-18%
- E-commerce: Open 15-22%, CTR 2-4%, CTOR 8-14%
- Media/Publishing: Open 22-32%, CTR 3-6%, CTOR 12-20%
- B2C Services: Open 18-25%, CTR 2-4%, CTOR 10-16%

Good: "Open rate: 24.2% (in line with B2B SaaS benchmark of 20-28%). CTR: 3.8% (above benchmark of 2-5%). CTOR: 15.7% (strong — content is resonating with openers). Note: Open rate adjusted for Apple MPP by excluding non-interactive opens."
Bad: "Our open rate is 24%."

**List Health**
The health of your list determines the ceiling of your email program. A list with 60% inactive subscribers is dragging down deliverability and inflating costs. Track total vs active (opened or clicked in 90 days), new subscriber acquisition rate, and list growth rate. Negative list growth (more unsubscribes than new subscribers) is a crisis.

Good: "Total list: 34,200. Active (90-day): 22,800 (66.7%). Inactive (90-day): 11,400 (33.3%). New subscribers this month: 1,840. Unsubscribes: 420. Net list growth: +4.2%. Action: The 11,400 inactive subscribers should enter a re-engagement flow. If they do not engage within 30 days, suppress or remove them to protect sender reputation."
Bad: "Our list is 34,000."

**Segment Performance**
The aggregate email metrics lie. Segment performance reveals which audiences are engaged and which are dragging down the average. At minimum, segment by: customers vs non-customers, subscriber tenure (new vs mature), engagement level (active vs at-risk vs inactive), and acquisition source. Include revenue per subscriber to identify which segments generate the most value.

Good: "Customers (8,200 subscribers): 32% open rate, 6.1% CTR, $4.20 rev/subscriber. Newsletter-only (18,000 subscribers): 18% open rate, 1.8% CTR, $0.12 rev/subscriber. Customers generate 35x more revenue per subscriber than newsletter-only subscribers."
Bad: Showing only overall metrics with no segmentation.

**A/B Test Results**
Every test must include: the variable tested, both variants described, the winning variant, the statistical confidence level, the lift percentage, and the next step. Do not report A/B test results without confidence levels — a "winner" at 60% confidence is noise, not a finding. Require 95% confidence minimum for implementation decisions.

Good: "Subject line test: 'Your checkout is losing revenue' (A) vs 'Cut cart abandonment by 34%' (B). Sent to 5,000 per variant. A: 22.4% open rate. B: 28.1% open rate. Winner: B (+25.4% lift, 98.2% confidence). Next step: Adopt specific-number subject lines for all product emails."
Bad: "We tested two subject lines and B won."

**Revenue Attribution**
Separate revenue by email type: campaign emails (one-time sends), automated flows (triggered sequences), and transactional emails (order confirmations that may include upsells). Automated flows typically generate 30-50% of email revenue despite being a small percentage of total sends. If your automated flows are generating less than 20% of email revenue, they are under-built.

Good: "Campaign emails: 98,000 sent, $42,400 revenue ($0.43/email). Automated flows: 26,000 sent, $43,800 revenue ($1.68/email). Flows generate 50.8% of email revenue from 21% of sends. Revenue per email is 3.9x higher for flows than campaigns."
Bad: "Email generated $86,200 this month."

### Conventions

- Always report open rate with a note on Apple Mail Privacy Protection (MPP) methodology. State whether MPP opens are included, excluded, or adjusted.
- Use click-through rate (CTR = clicks / delivered) and click-to-open rate (CTOR = clicks / opens) as separate metrics. They measure different things.
- Report both unique and total metrics where relevant. Unique clicks = how many people clicked. Total clicks = how engaged they were.
- List health must include the active/inactive split. A growing list with declining active percentage is a liability, not an asset.
- A/B test results require 95% statistical confidence to declare a winner. Anything below that is inconclusive.
- Revenue attribution must separate campaign revenue from automated flow revenue. They have different economics.
- Format: commas for thousands, one decimal place for percentages, dollar signs for revenue.
- Include benchmarks for every engagement metric. Without benchmarks, a 3% CTR has no meaning.
- Segment performance must include revenue per subscriber, not just engagement metrics. Engagement without revenue context is vanity.
- Automated flow performance should be reviewed separately from campaign performance — they have different optimization levers.
- The audience is the email marketing team, the broader marketing team, and leadership who allocate resources.
- Always include trend vs previous period for every metric. Absolute numbers without direction are insufficient.

### Anti-Patterns

- Do NOT report open rate as a reliable primary metric without acknowledging Apple MPP inflation. Open rate is directional for trending, not absolute for benchmarking.
- Do NOT ignore deliverability metrics. An email with a 30% open rate and a 92% delivery rate is reaching far fewer inboxes than it appears.
- Do NOT average engagement metrics across all email types. Campaign emails, automated flows, and transactional emails have fundamentally different engagement patterns.
- Do NOT report A/B test results without statistical confidence. A "winning" subject line at 62% confidence is indistinguishable from random chance.
- Do NOT treat unsubscribes as purely negative. An unsubscribe from a disengaged subscriber improves list health and deliverability. Unsubscribes from engaged subscribers are the real concern.
- Do NOT send to your entire list for every email. Segment by engagement level at minimum. Sending to inactive subscribers damages sender reputation.
- Do NOT ignore send frequency analysis. If unsubscribe rate spikes after a high-send week, you are over-mailing.
- Do NOT report list growth without netting out unsubscribes and bounces. "We added 2,000 subscribers" is meaningless if 1,800 unsubscribed and 400 bounced.
- Do NOT compare email engagement across different send types without noting the context. A product launch email and a monthly newsletter will have very different engagement profiles.
- Do NOT ignore the time-of-send analysis. The same email sent on Tuesday at 10am vs Saturday at 6pm will have dramatically different results.
- Do NOT report revenue attribution without specifying the attribution window and model. "Email generated $86K" could be 24-hour last-click or 30-day multi-touch — the difference is significant.
- Do NOT skip list hygiene reviews. An email list degrades at approximately 25-30% per year through job changes, abandoned accounts, and spam traps.
- Do NOT build your email program around campaign blasts alone. Automated flows generate higher revenue per email and require less ongoing effort.
- Do NOT ignore mobile rendering performance. 60%+ of emails are opened on mobile. If your emails are not mobile-optimized, you are losing clicks.
- Do NOT report engagement metrics without segmenting by subscriber tenure. New subscribers (first 30 days) typically engage at 2-3x the rate of mature subscribers — combining them inflates the average.

### Customization

Replace all bracket sections with your program's specifics. For B2B SaaS, replace revenue attribution with pipeline attribution and track demo requests, trial starts, and MQLs instead of purchases. For media and publishing, replace revenue metrics with engagement depth (articles read, time on site from email clicks) and subscriber retention. For non-profit, track donation conversion rate and average gift size instead of AOV. For marketplaces, create separate email analytics for buyers and sellers. Adjust benchmarks to your industry — B2B SaaS open rates are typically 5-8pp higher than e-commerce, and CTOR benchmarks differ accordingly. For transactional email (order confirmations, shipping updates), track separately and measure upsell/cross-sell conversion within those emails.
