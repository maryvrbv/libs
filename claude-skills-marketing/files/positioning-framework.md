## Skill: Positioning Framework

A positioning framework defines how your product or brand occupies a distinct place in your target audience's mind. Marketers write this when launching a new product, entering a new market, or when current messaging isn't converting. It answers: "Why should this specific audience choose us over every other option, including doing nothing?"

### How to Write Each Section

**Category Definition**
Name the category your audience already searches for. If you're creating a new category, you need a very good reason — creating a category means spending years educating the market before you can sell to it. For most products, positioning within an existing category with a clear differentiator is more effective.

Good: "Beamline competes in the marketing analytics platform category — software that helps marketing teams measure campaign performance, attribute revenue to channels, and report on ROI."

Bad: "Beamline is in the marketing technology space." (Too broad. "MarTech" contains 10,000+ products.)

**Target Audience**
Be ruthlessly specific. "Marketing leaders" is too broad. "VP of Marketing at B2B SaaS companies with 50-200 employees who are currently using Google Analytics and spreadsheets to report on marketing ROI" is positioning-grade specificity. Include the buying trigger — the specific event that makes someone start looking for a solution. That's when your positioning has to land.

**Competitive Alternatives**
Include direct competitors, indirect competitors, and the status quo. The status quo (doing nothing, using spreadsheets, hiring a consultant) is often your biggest competitor. Be honest about where competitors are stronger — your positioning doesn't need to win on every dimension, just the ones that matter most to your target audience.

**Unique Differentiation**
This is the hardest section. Your differentiation must be: (1) true — you can actually prove it, (2) relevant — the audience cares about it, and (3) defensible — competitors can't easily copy it. "Better UX" is almost never defensible. "The only platform that integrates with Shopify's real-time event stream to attribute revenue within 5 minutes of purchase" is.

**Value Proposition**
Use the formula: For [audience] who [situation], [Product] is the [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [alternative], we [differentiator]. This is the most tested positioning formula in marketing — it forces clarity by making you fill in every variable.

**Proof Points**
Positioning without proof is just claims. Every benefit needs evidence. Customer results are the most persuasive, followed by product benchmarks, followed by third-party validation. Specific numbers beat vague superlatives.

**Messaging Guardrails**
Define what you say and what you don't. This prevents messaging drift over time. Include the "why" for things you avoid — "We don't say 'AI-powered' because every competitor says it and it's meaningless as a differentiator."

### Conventions

- Length: 2-4 pages
- Tone: Precise and strategic. This is an internal alignment document — it should read like strategy, not ad copy
- The value proposition formula (For/Who/Is the/That/Unlike/We) is required — it's the most important sentence in the document
- Every differentiation claim must have at least one proof point. No unsupported claims
- Include the status quo as a competitive alternative — ignoring it means you don't understand your buyer's real decision
- Review this document when you launch a new product, enter a new market, or when win rates drop below target
- The positioning statement is internal-facing — it's the source of truth, not customer-facing copy
- Name the buying trigger for each audience — this tells you when and where your positioning needs to appear
- One positioning framework per product or per distinct audience — don't combine B2B and B2C positioning in one doc
- Link to the messaging architecture document for channel-specific copy guidance

### Anti-Patterns

- Do NOT confuse positioning with messaging — positioning is the strategic foundation (what you stand for); messaging is the execution (what you say). Write positioning first
- Do NOT list features as differentiation — "We have API access" is a feature. "We're the only platform that lets you build custom attribution models without engineering support" is differentiation
- Do NOT claim differentiation you can't prove — if your unique differentiator is "we're easier to use," you need usability test data, not just your product team's opinion
- Do NOT skip the competitive alternatives section — if you don't name what you're positioning against, you're positioning in a vacuum
- Do NOT position against every competitor — pick the 2-3 alternatives your target audience is actually evaluating
- Do NOT use vague superlatives — "best-in-class," "industry-leading," and "cutting-edge" communicate nothing. Be specific
- Do NOT make the positioning statement customer-facing — it's too dense. The messaging architecture translates positioning into copy
- Do NOT create a positioning framework by consensus — strong positioning requires making choices that exclude people. If everyone agrees with the positioning, it's probably too safe
- Do NOT ignore the status quo — for most B2B products, the biggest competitor is "do nothing" or "use a spreadsheet"
- Do NOT position on price alone — unless you have a structural cost advantage, someone will always undercut you
- Do NOT assume your category is obvious — define it explicitly so everyone on the team uses the same language
- Do NOT write positioning that tries to appeal to everyone — the best positioning repels the wrong audience as clearly as it attracts the right one

### Examples

```markdown
# Positioning Framework: Meridian Analytics

**Author:** Kai Nakamura
**Last Updated:** 2026-03-20
**Status:** Approved

---

## 1. Category Definition

**Category:** Marketing Attribution Platform
**Category Description:** Software that connects marketing spend to revenue outcomes, helping marketing teams understand which channels, campaigns, and content drive pipeline and closed deals.
**Category Maturity:** Growing — the category exists but is evolving rapidly as privacy changes break legacy attribution models and AI-native approaches emerge.

## 2. Target Audience

### Primary Audience
- **Title/Role:** VP of Marketing or Director of Demand Generation
- **Company Profile:** B2B SaaS, Series B-D, 100-500 employees, $10M-$100M ARR
- **Situation:** Spending $200K-$2M/year on marketing and struggling to prove ROI to their CEO and board. Current attribution setup (GA4 + CRM reports) undercounts marketing's contribution by 40-60%.
- **Primary Pain:** Cannot connect marketing spend to pipeline and revenue in a way leadership trusts, which threatens marketing budget in the next planning cycle.
- **Current Solution:** Google Analytics for web, Salesforce reports for pipeline, manual spreadsheets to stitch them together. 15+ hours/month building attribution reports.
- **Buying Trigger:** Board or CEO asks "what's our CAC by channel?" and the VP of Marketing can't answer with confidence.

### Secondary Audience
- **Title/Role:** Marketing Operations Manager or Marketing Analyst
- **Company Profile:** Same as above
- **Situation:** Building custom attribution reports in Looker or Tableau, maintaining brittle data pipelines, and spending 60% of their time on reporting instead of analysis.
- **Primary Pain:** Lacks a reliable, automated system for multi-touch attribution — every report is a manual project.
- **Current Solution:** Custom SQL queries + Looker dashboards + spreadsheets. Takes 3-5 days to produce an attribution report.
- **Buying Trigger:** A data pipeline breaks before a board meeting, or they're asked to produce a new attribution model and estimate it'll take 4 weeks.

## 3. Competitive Alternatives

| Alternative | Type | Strength Against Us | Weakness Against Us |
|-------------|------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Bizible (Marketo) | Direct competitor | Deep Marketo integration, Adobe ecosystem lock-in, established brand | Expensive ($40K+/yr), complex implementation (3-6 months), legacy architecture not built for privacy-first world |
| HockeyStack | Direct competitor | Fast setup, modern UI, good content marketing driving awareness | Limited enterprise features, attribution model not transparent (black box), 2 years younger |
| Dreamdata | Direct competitor | Strong B2B focus, revenue attribution for account-based models | European-centric, limited US customer base, weaker CRM integrations |
| Google Analytics 4 + spreadsheets | Status quo | Free, familiar, already implemented | No multi-touch attribution, no revenue connection, 15+ hours/month manual work, leadership doesn't trust the numbers |

## 4. Unique Differentiation

**What we do that no one else does:**
Meridian is the only attribution platform that combines deterministic multi-touch attribution with AI-powered incrementality testing, giving marketing leaders both the "what happened" and the "what actually caused revenue" — in a single view, with zero data engineering required.

**Why this matters to the target audience:**
VPs of Marketing need numbers leadership trusts. Attribution alone shows correlation. Incrementality shows causation. Having both in one platform means they can defend their budget with data that holds up to CFO scrutiny.

**Evidence:**
- 94% of Meridian customers report increased confidence in marketing ROI reporting within 60 days (2025 customer survey, n=127)
- Average implementation time: 14 days vs. industry average of 90+ days for Bizible
- 3 of the top 10 fastest-growing B2B SaaS companies (2025 Deloitte Fast 500) use Meridian for board-level attribution reporting

## 5. Value Proposition

**For** VPs of Marketing at B2B SaaS companies ($10M-$100M ARR)
**who** can't confidently connect marketing spend to pipeline and revenue,
**Meridian** is the marketing attribution platform
**that** proves marketing's true impact with both attribution and incrementality in one view.
**Unlike** Bizible or manual spreadsheet analysis,
**we** deliver board-ready attribution reports in 14 days with no data engineering required — and prove not just what correlated with revenue, but what caused it.

## 6. Key Benefits

| Benefit | Supporting Feature | Proof Point |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|
| Defend your budget with numbers leadership trusts | Incrementality testing built into every attribution report | 72% of customers report successfully defending or increasing marketing budget at next board review |
| Stop spending 15 hours/month building attribution reports | Automated multi-touch attribution with CRM sync | Average customer saves 18 hours/month on attribution reporting (self-reported, n=89) |
| Go live in 2 weeks, not 3 months | No-code setup with pre-built integrations for Salesforce, HubSpot, and major ad platforms | Median time to first report: 11 days. No customer has required data engineering support for implementation |

## 7. Proof Points

### Customer Evidence
- Lattice (HR SaaS, $100M ARR): "Meridian showed us that our podcast sponsorships were driving 3x more pipeline than our Google Ads — something GA4 completely missed. We reallocated $400K in Q3 and saw a 22% reduction in blended CAC."
- Clearbit (Data SaaS, acquired by HubSpot): Used Meridian to prove content marketing's contribution to pipeline, resulting in a 40% increase in content team headcount.

### Product Evidence
- 23 pre-built integrations (CRM, ad platforms, marketing automation) — no custom API work needed
- Attribution reports refresh every 4 hours automatically
- Incrementality testing runs as a background process — no manual experiment setup required

### Third-Party Evidence
- Named a "Strong Performer" in Forrester Wave for Marketing Measurement, Q1 2026
- Featured in First Round Review: "How Growing SaaS Companies Measure Marketing ROI"
- SOC 2 Type II certified

## 8. Positioning Statement

Meridian is the marketing attribution platform built for B2B SaaS marketing leaders who need to prove — not just report — marketing's impact on revenue. While legacy tools like Bizible require six-figure contracts and months of implementation, and manual approaches (GA4 + spreadsheets) produce numbers nobody trusts, Meridian delivers board-ready attribution with built-in incrementality testing in 14 days, with no data engineering required. We serve the VP of Marketing who's tired of defending their budget with correlation data and wants causation-level proof that holds up to CFO scrutiny. Our customers don't just know what happened — they know what actually drove revenue.

## 9. Messaging Guardrails

### We Say
- "Prove marketing's impact" — not "track" or "report on." We're about proof, not dashboards.
- "Board-ready" — our reports are designed for the audience that matters: leadership.
- "Attribution + incrementality" — this is our differentiator. Always pair them.
- "14 days to first report" — speed of implementation is a competitive weapon. Use the specific number.
- "No data engineering required" — this removes the biggest objection and differentiates from Bizible.

### We Don't Say
- "AI-powered" as a leading claim — every competitor says this. We mention AI in the context of incrementality testing, not as a headline.
- "Single source of truth" — overused cliche that means nothing. Instead: "the report your CFO actually trusts."
- "Real-time attribution" — our data refreshes every 4 hours. We don't claim real-time because B2B buying cycles don't require it, and overclaiming erodes trust.
- "Enterprise-grade" — we serve mid-market. Claiming enterprise invites comparison with Bizible's feature set, where we'd lose.

### Tone
Confident and specific. We sound like a senior marketing leader who's been in the room when the CEO questions marketing spend — because our founders have been. We lead with numbers, not adjectives. We respect the audience's intelligence and don't oversimplify.
```

### Customization

Replace all `[bracketed]` sections with your product's specifics. The value proposition formula (For/Who/Is the/That/Unlike/We) is the most critical section — spend the most time here. The competitive alternatives table should include 3-5 entries including the status quo. Proof points should be updated quarterly as you gather more customer evidence. If you don't have customer evidence yet (pre-launch), use product benchmarks and founder credibility as proof.
